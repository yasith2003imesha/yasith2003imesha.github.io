About me

Hello, I am Yasith Imesha, a first-year BSc (Hons) Biotechnology undergraduate at SLIIT University. I grew up in Haliela, Badulla, and completed my G.C.E. Ordinary Level and Advanced Level (Biological Science stream) at Badulla Central College.

My curiosity about living things began in the school science laboratory, where I volunteered as a lab assistant, and it has grown ever since. I enjoy practical work such as DNA/RNA extraction, PCR and gel electrophoresis, and I like turning difficult biology into simple explanations, which I practised as a volunteer peer tutor and at my school science exhibition.

I am careful, disciplined in the lab, and happy to work on my own or in a team. I am now looking for an internship in genetics where I can support real research, build my laboratory and bioinformatics skills, and improve my scientific writing. Outside my studies, I enjoy films, books and badminton.

Coursework: Introduction to Biotechnology (SC1153)

These are the four assignments I completed in my first-semester biotechnology module at SLIIT, with a short summary and reflection for each. The full write-up is in my assessment portfolio.

View full assessment portfolio (PDF)

Assignment 1: Gray Biotechnology (Group Presentation)

Presentation title: "Nature's Silent Shield" | Group of four | Presenter 3

Our group presented how biotechnology protects the environment. I covered Waste-to-Energy (incineration, anaerobic digestion, gasification and pyrolysis, with their advantages and limits) and Biofilters (how microorganisms in compost, soil, wood chips or activated carbon remove pollutants such as ammonia, hydrogen sulfide and odours).

What I learned: how to explain technical ideas simply, keep a smooth hand-over between speakers and manage my time.

View presentation slides

Assignment 2: Biotechnology Product Video (Group Project)

Topic: Producing egg white protein using biotechnology | Group of four

We made a video for a general audience about egg white protein produced by biotechnology. My section explained the advantages of this approach, such as controlled production, consistent quality and scalable output, and its future benefits for alternative proteins and food biotechnology.

What I learned: how to communicate science to people without a biotechnology background and how to coordinate recording and editing as a team.

Watch the video

Assignment 3: Career Trajectory in Biotechnology (Individual Presentation)

Role model: Professor Frances H. Arnold

I researched the career of Nobel Prize winner Frances H. Arnold, known for directed evolution of enzymes, and used it to plan my own path. My plan runs from building foundations in molecular biology, biochemistry, genetics, lab techniques and bioinformatics (2026-2027), to an undergraduate research project and internship (2027-2029), and then postgraduate study and a research scientist career (2030 onwards).

What I learned: a career needs research experience, practical skills and persistence, not only a degree. My own formula: Knowledge + Skills + Research + Persistence = Career Growth.

View presentation slides

Assignment 4: Ethical Debate on Genetic Privacy and Law Enforcement

Debate motion: should police use consumer DNA databases to trace suspects through relatives? | Opposition team, third speaker

In round one I argued about the privacy and security risks of commercial genetic databases: private ownership, data breaches, the fact that DNA cannot be changed like a password, and misuse beyond the original purpose. In round two I answered the other team, accepting that genetic genealogy can help solve crimes but arguing that this does not remove the privacy and consent concerns of relatives.

Key message: "I am not against DNA technology; I am against uncontrolled access to private DNA data."

What I learned: to listen carefully, rebut the strongest opposing point and stay respectful while arguing with evidence.
